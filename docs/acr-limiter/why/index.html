ACR LIMITER

One gain, a clipper, or every half-wave

How ACR LIMITER differs from an ordinary limiter, a clipper and a multiband limiter.

Updated 19 November 2026

What is ACR LIMITER?

ACR LIMITER is a mastering limiter plugin (VST3) by the makers of HOUZY. One AMOUNT knob raises the level, and four stages catch the peaks in order, each taking only the part it handles best: the ACR clipper takes the short peaks above ~4 kHz, the T8 / T9 limiter holds the bass, the kick body and the middle, six linear-phase bands keep a bass peak from ducking the highs, and TRUE PEAK guards the ceiling between samples.

One gain for the whole peak.

An ordinary limiter is output = gain × signal, with the gain pulled down whenever any peak is too loud. Every classic complaint follows from that:

  • The kick ducks the highs — a bass peak pulls the hats, the vocal and the reverb tails down with it. That is pumping at the very end of the chain.
  • Grit inside every hit — the gain moves in the middle of a bass wave and bends it; around a 50 Hz kick you hear it as a dirty, crunchy low end.
  • Dull top end — a hard clipper slices the waveform; its error is broadband and lands on the hats and the air.
  • Inter-sample overs — the export reads −0.1 dBFS, then clips after MP3 or streaming encoding.

The usual answer is to stack a clipper and a limiter and hope they do not fight. ACR LIMITER builds the two into one chain where each stage takes only its part: the clipper never sees the bass, the bass limiter never moves inside a wave, and the bands keep one peak from pulling the rest of the mix down.

A clipper or a multiband limiter helps, but only half way

A clipper keeps the punch and adds loudness, but its slice is broadband: push it and the bass crunches and the top goes dull. A multiband limiter stops the kick from ducking the hats, but inside each band one gain still moves in the middle of the wave. ACR LIMITER takes the clipper off the bass, and moves the bass gain only where the wave crosses zero.

Louder without getting dirty.

ACR subtracts a short pulse placed exactly at the peak instead of slicing it. Its spectrum puts the distortion at the top, where the hats and the attack mask it; its depth grows with AMOUNT, from 0.5 dB to 2 dB.

T8 changes its gain only where the wave crosses zero: each half-wave of the kick and the bass is lowered as one whole piece, so its shape stays exactly as it was. T9 follows the envelope of the bass and lets long sub notes go smoothly, with no flutter. One switch, a 10 ms crossfade, T9 by default.

CEILING, CHARACTER (light saturation before the limiter) and OUTPUT set the rest. BYPASS is aligned to the latency, so you compare the same moment.

When another limiter is better

Tracking and live playing, where every millisecond of latency matters. ACR LIMITER looks 0.1 s ahead and is made for the master bus and the final loudness.

Questions people ask

Does ACR LIMITER pump?

Much less than a one-gain limiter: the bands are limited one by one, so a bass peak does not pull the highs down, and the bass gain moves only at zero crossings.

T8 or T9?

Both hold the low end. T8 keeps every half-wave whole; T9 follows the envelope and is smoother on long sub notes. Switch while the track plays and choose by ear.

Can I use it with HOUZY?

Yes: HOUZY first for even compression, ACR LIMITER last for the loudness and the ceiling. Two plugins, two licences.

Which formats and DAWs?

VST3 on Windows 10/11, 64-bit: Ableton Live, FL Studio, Cubase, Reaper, Bitwig and other VST3 hosts.
